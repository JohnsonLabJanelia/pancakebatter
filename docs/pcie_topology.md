# PCIe host-bridge topology of pancake0

Which devices share a root complex decides whose traffic competes. The record
lives in `hosts/pancake0/config.yml` under `pcie_topology` (generated by
`pcie_topology.py --record`, notes by hand) and `pcie_topology.py --check`
verifies the live host against it. Measured 2026-10-09.

| Host bridge | Devices | Shared links worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| `00` | RTX A6000 (GPU0, display/GUI) | alone |
| `20` | A16 card **A** = GPU1-4 (2f-32:00.0, behind the card's PCIe switch 2b/2c/2d/2e); **all three NVMe**: nvme2 29:00.0 = OS (`/`, `/boot/efi`), nvme1 2a:00.0 = `/mnt/Data1`, nvme0 23:00.0 = `/mnt/Data2`; **both Intel X550T 10 GbE ports** 24:00.0 = `ethernet_1` (lab uplink, default route), 24:00.1 = `ethernet_2` (PDU/control 192.168.20.x) | nvme0 (`/mnt/Data2`) and the X550T sit behind one x8 chipset link, root port `20:01.1` → `21:00.0` → `22:00.0`/`22:02.0`. Data1 and the OS drive have their own x4 root ports (`20:01.3`, `20:01.2`). Card A is x16 on `20:03.1`. |
| `40` | A16 card **B** = GPU5-8 (45-48:00.0); ConnectX-7 **mlnx2** 49:00.0-3 (cameras 2010095, 2010096 on p3/p4; p1/p2 spare) | card x16 on `40:01.1`, NIC x16 on `40:03.1` |
| `60` | ConnectX-7 **mlnx1** 61:00.0-3 (cameras 2010093, 2010094 on p1/p2; 2012856 on p3; p4 spare) | NIC x16 on `60:01.1`; the card's frames land on card A, i.e. they cross to bridge 20 |

## What follows from it

- **Bridge 20 is where contention happens.** It carries the GPUs that the
  mlnx1 cameras DMA into, every byte written to or read from disk, and every
  byte that leaves the machine for the lab network or the PDU. The writeback
  stalls of 2026-09 (recorders writing ~600 MB/s, the page cache flushing
  6 GB/s bursts) were this bridge; `vm.dirty_background_bytes`/`vm.dirty_bytes`
  in [`kernel_tuning.md`](kernel_tuning.md) are the mitigation.
- **Bulk network egress shares bridge 20 with card A and all storage.**
  Copying clips to `/groups` while recording would read from Data1 or Data2
  and send on `ethernet_1`: both halves on bridge 20, and if the source is
  `/mnt/Data2` the read and the send also share the one x8 link under
  `20:01.1`. Such a job needs a measured no-impact proof before it runs during
  acquisition (frame cadence and `rig_health_check.py` history during a soak).
- **There is no second route out that avoids bridge 20.** `ethernet_2` is the
  other port of the same X550T chip. The only other NICs are the ConnectX-7
  ports on bridges 40 and 60; a spare one (mlnx2 p1/p2 on 40, mlnx1 p4 on 60)
  could carry a fibre uplink off bridge 20, but it would then share a camera
  card's bridge and PCIe link, which is a different conflict, not none. An
  extra NIC in a free slot on bridge 00 (beside the A6000) is the clean option;
  the slot table is not recorded yet (install the root probe and refresh to
  capture `system_info.pcie_slots`).
- **Drives are identified by serial and filesystem UUID, never by `nvmeN`.**
  The kernel's `nvmeN` numbering changes between boots (today nvme0 = Data2,
  nvme1 = Data1, nvme2 = OS; after the 2026-10-06 reboot it differed from the
  recorded order). The topology block therefore carries each drive's
  controller serial and its partitions' UUIDs with their mounts, `--check`
  follows a drive by serial (so it can tell a drive that moved slot from one
  that was renamed), `storage_devices` matches by serial on refresh and now
  records partition UUIDs, and `/etc/fstab` mounts by UUID. Serials: 219 = Data2
  (bb4ea7ac...), 297 = Data1 (4b655567...), 222 = OS (c60c38ed...).

## Keeping it current

```bash
./pcie_topology.py            # live table with the switch path of every device
./pcie_topology.py --check    # live vs recorded; run after moving a card or adding a NIC
./pcie_topology.py --record   # regenerate the block (notes are kept); paste over the section in config.yml
```
